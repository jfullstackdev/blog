Crypto, As I Learned It

December 27, 2025

All about my take on Crypto : from my very first encounters, to understanding and actually using Crypto, including trading and investing strategies.

This may be a local discussion focused on the Philippines, but most of the concepts here apply broadly to Crypto in general.

This is not meant to be a step-by-step guide or financial advice, but a personal record of lessons, mistakes, and realizations gathered along the way while navigating the Crypto space.

My First Encounters

Way back then, when I first heard about Bitcoin and Crypto, my initial thought was that it had something to do with cryptography. Well, in those times, the shortened term “Crypto” was more closely associated with cryptography: encryption, security, and complex algorithms, rather than currencies or assets. These days, however, Crypto almost always refers to Cryptocurrency.

At the time, tech YouTubers talked a lot about these topics, people like TechLead and JomaTech. Even if they were not my main content targets, I absorbed bits and pieces of information. All I really understood back then was that people could gain money through trading, much like earning interest when someone owes you.

As a Dev, I find it similar when we want to learn coding or a new Tech Stack. At first it's very vague, fragmented knowledge that later on, would make sense. So, I guess that helped me dealing with Crypto.

Then in 2025, just this December, I decided to finally take learning Crypto seriously. I realized that, fortunately, in the Philippines there are local and approved platforms available. At the same time, there were also restrictions, such as the banning of Binance in 2024.

This mix of accessibility and limitation turned out to be both good and bad, something I will tackle later in this write-up.

High Risk, High Reward

First of all, it should be clear that gaining significant money is not possible without taking proportionate risk.

If that were not the case, everyone could simply work for a short time, put money into trading or investing, and earn easily. That kind of system would not make sense, because money only has value when it is scarce and tied to effort and risk.

So for those who are uncomfortable with the idea of losing money, it is better to think twice before entering Crypto, or at least accept this reality from the start.

Another thing that often gets overlooked is not just whether you put money into Crypto, but how much you put in. Small amounts can still grow, but the impact is usually limited. For example, if I put 10K PHP into Bitcoin and it gains 10 percent, that is only 1K PHP, roughly a day’s work for many office jobs.

In Metro Manila, even a relatively cheap studio-type apartment can cost around 8K to 10K PHP per month. To reach that level of impact from a 10 percent gain, the base capital would already need to be closer to 100K PHP. In the end, how much risk and capital to commit is a personal decision, and the expectations should match the size of the investment / trading.

Also, for those who are looking for something closer to a gambling experience ( yes, the thrill ! ) but do not want to deal with actual gambling, Crypto can fill that role. This usually happens with highly hyped and very volatile coins.

In these cases, some people choose to treat them as entertainment rather than investment, watching price movements and entering with amounts they are fully prepared to lose. As long as this is done with clear limits and expectations, it becomes more about controlled risk-taking than blind speculation. Gamble responsibly !

The Tools Needed

When I started using Maya PH Banking and GCash, I noticed that both already offered Crypto services. This made my curiosity grow even more, and I initially wanted to put money in right away. However, before doing that, I searched first, particularly on Reddit, to read about other Pinoys’ experiences.

What I learned early on was that these tools are called Exchanges. They are platforms where you buy, sell, and convert Crypto. Without exchanges, it is difficult to actually transact or move value.

From those discussions, I also saw that some platforms were often considered unreliable, and that better options were frequently recommended, such as PDAX and Coins.ph. Others also mentioned using Binance and similar platforms through VPNs.

International or Local Exchanges

As mentioned earlier, restricting international exchanges comes with both good and bad outcomes. On one hand, this means complying with Philippine government regulations, which many people on Reddit strongly dislike. After all, one of the original ideas behind Crypto is to operate independently of government control.

On the other hand, regulation also brings certain protections. Locally regulated exchanges are secured under the BSP, which means that if something goes wrong, there is at least a higher chance of recovering funds. This is similar to how registered digital banks and e-wallets operate in the Philippines.

So in reality, Crypto as a purely decentralized system did not fully play out, even on a global scale. We see major platforms like Binance being banned or regulated in many countries.

That said, some parts of Crypto remain truly decentralized. The blockchain itself does not depend on any single authority, and if you really want to, you can still hold your own money without relying on exchanges or intermediaries.

Trading Strats

My approach to trading is closer to swing trading rather than short-term scalping.

For Bitcoin, I usually place a sell limit order once it gains around 8 to 10 percent. For Ethereum, the target is slightly higher, around 15 to 17 percent. With Solana, which is more volatile, I aim for around 20 to 25 percent gains.

I also do not sell everything at once. Most of the time, I only sell a portion, usually around 20 to 25 percent, while keeping the rest of the position intact. This allows me to lock in some profit without fully exiting the trade.

When it comes to stop losses, I only apply them to Solana. If the price dips hard, around 25 percent or more, I sell only the same portion instead of closing the entire position. This helps manage risk while still staying exposed in case of a recovery.

Asset Profit Target Portion Sold Stop Loss Used
Bitcoin 8–10% 20–25% No
Ethereum 15–17% 20–25% No
Solana 20–25% 20–25% Yes

Coins Pro

One feature of Coins.ph is Coins Pro, which works similarly to most major exchanges.

Here, even buying can be done through limit orders, giving you more control over the price compared to simple market buys.

For deeper transactions and stablecoins, using a limit order when buying does not always make a significant difference. However, the option becomes more useful when dealing with volatile assets.

That said, Coins Pro is not basic at all. Using it properly requires learning how exchanges work, including order types, pricing, and market behavior.

At this level, you start seeing concepts like order books, bid and ask prices, and spreads. This is also where trading begins to feel less like a simple app feature and more like an actual market.

Fees, Spread, and the Hidden Costs

With Coins Pro, you gain more control compared to simple buy and sell options, but this also exposes costs that are not always obvious at first glance.

Aside from trading fees, there is the spread — the difference between the buy and sell price. Even if fees look small, spreads can quietly eat into profits, especially when trading frequently or with small amounts.

These hidden costs become more noticeable in low-liquidity pairs, where prices move more easily and orders may not fill at the expected levels. This is why buying and selling can sometimes feel unfair, even when the market itself is functioning normally.

Understanding fees and spreads is important because they affect results over time. In Crypto, it is often not just about being right on direction, but also about managing these small costs that add up.

Cost Type What It Is Why It Matters
Trading Fee Fee per executed trade Reduces profit on every entry and exit
Spread Difference between buy and sell price Hidden cost, especially on small trades
Slippage Price change during execution Common in low-liquidity pairs
Liquidity How easily trades are filled Low liquidity increases spread and slippage

Investing

When it comes to investing, we know that Bitcoin and Ethereum are the core assets.

Bitcoin tends to follow a rough four-year cycle, while Ethereum often moves with broader market trends and ecosystem growth.

In investing, I consider day-to-day price movements as mostly noise. My positions are based on long-term conviction rather than short-term fluctuations.

Because of this, I use Bitcoin and Ethereum in both trading and investing, so these two major coins serve two different purposes for me.

To properly separate them, I do not keep long-term holdings on Coins.ph. Instead, I move them to hot wallets or cold wallets for storage.

Investing Proper Storage

As mentioned above, in investing, buying is only the first step. After that, proper storage becomes just as important. This is where hot wallets and cold wallets come into play.

Hot wallets are usually connected to the internet and are more convenient for frequent access. Cold wallets, on the other hand, are kept offline and are mainly used for long-term storage.

Fortunately, in the Philippines, there is already a reliable local distributor of Ledger, which makes using a hardware wallet more accessible.

Some tips I often see from other Pinoys include using an old phone dedicated only to Crypto, or being extremely careful with manual storage of recovery phrases. These may sound simple, but they go a long way in reducing unnecessary risk.

Parking Money

One thing I found cool was the idea of putting money into Crypto without actively trading. It's like I have money but on Crypto !

My first choice was to buy USDC ( because I use USDT for trading ) and leave it untouched. When the time comes, I can sell it back to pesos if the peso loses value, for example from 59 PHP to 65 PHP. In that sense, it feels similar to long-term Forex exposure.

The downside is that the gains are not that significant. In many cases, a traditional time deposit can still outperform this strategy.

Because of that, I also looked into launchpools and reward programs. For example, holding PHPC can earn around 3 percent annually, and I often hear that rates used to be as high as 8 percent before.

Then I encountered Tether Gold ! Even in 2025, it performed very well, with gains reported at more than 50 percent. This was not due to hype or a pump, but because it closely tracks the real value of gold. In a way, this addressed my need for a more stable, commodity-backed option.

I do hope that more commodities become successfully pegged to coins in the future. For now, what we mostly have are stablecoins, Tether Gold, and silver-backed tokens, although silver still suffers from low liquidity.

Option Purpose Risk Level
USDC or USDT Preserve value vs PHP Low
PHPC Rewards Earn modest yield Low to medium
Tether Gold Commodity exposure Medium

Liquidity in PH Trading

Based on my experience using Coins.ph, trading pairs in PHP are the most numerous and the most commonly traded. These are followed by USDT pairs, which are available for fewer assets, and lastly USDC pairs, which have the least coverage on the platform.

Unfortunately, Tether Gold is still not paired with USDT. Because of this, liquidity remains limited compared to major stablecoin pairs. I actually suggested this pairing to Coins.ph Support, but they said that this is not something they are planning to implement at this time.

Pairing Tether Gold with USDT would, in my opinion, significantly improve liquidity and make it more practical to trade within the platform.

My Personal Rules

From all of these learnings, I ended up with a few simple rules that guide how I use and enjoy Crypto.

  • for trading, I only use money I am prepared to lose
  • for investing, I only commit money I do not need in the near term and can hold through volatility
  • treat trading as a tool, not a guaranteed source of income
  • never chase pumps or sudden hype, no matter how tempting unless you want gamble-style fun
  • I sell in portions and avoid going all in or all out
  • I respect volatility and never assume the market owes me profits
  • I prioritize liquidity, especially when dealing with local platforms in the Philippines
  • I do not overtrade; sometimes doing nothing is the best move
  • I separate long-term investing from short-term trading
  • I take security seriously and never rush wallet-related actions
  • I assume every link, message, and offer could be a scam
  • I stay realistic with yields and avoid anything that sounds too good to be true

enjoy Crypto as fun and earning !
